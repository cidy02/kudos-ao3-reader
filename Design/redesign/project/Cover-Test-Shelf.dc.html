C · Adopted: every choice has a reason

A reader’s shelf label, drawn by the app from what the AO3 listing already gives it. Rating, warnings, category, pairing and popularity stay in the symbols and meta line. Second line: twelve more Sherlock Holmes one-shots. The grey line under each cover says which rules it got. Drawn by CoverArt, the component every board in the canvas now uses for covers.

Fandom → colour familyFandoms from one source share a family: Persuasion and Pride and Prejudice are both Austen green.
Series → one shared lookSame motif, ground and angle for every part. The hue steps with each Part number.
Title → text placementShort titles sit big at the top, long ones at the bottom, subtitled ones right-aligned. The motif takes the space left.
Tone tags → motif familySoft, angular, orderly or celestial, from a short list of AO3 tags. Mixed or no tone tags stay neutral.
Author → motif and title styleWhich motif within the family, and serif, italic or small caps: a house style per writer.
Length → how full the motif isA short work gets the sparest version of its motif; each step up adds more of it — more rays, rings or waves — never a second copy on top.
Year posted → depth of shadeOlder works are darker, newer ones lighter.
Status → ringA ring around the motif: closed when the work is complete, open while it is in progress.
Crossover → 60/40 splitAlways the same diagonal. The first-listed fandom gets the larger part.
Work ID → the leftoversRotation, scale, ground and line weight only, so two works never match exactly.
