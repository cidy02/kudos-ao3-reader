The Cartographer's Daughter

By noon the salt flats opened in front of them, flat and bright as a held breath. Elinor knelt and pressed her palm to the crust; it gave a little, the way new ice does.

“Your mother crossed here,” Tomas said. It wasn't a question.

“Twice.” She unrolled the vellum and weighted the corners with stones. “Once going out. Once coming home with me.”

She drew the first line of the flats as a single stroke, left to right, without lifting the pen. It was the only honest way to draw a place that had no landmarks: you committed to the horizon and hoped it held.

Chapter 7 · The Salt Road

They walked until the light went copper. Somewhere behind them the dunes had folded themselves away, and ahead there was nothing but the white ground and the long blue shadow each of them threw.

“How will you know where to stop?” Tomas asked.

Elinor checked the compass. The needle was steady, which was its own kind of answer. “When the map runs out,” she said, “I'll know I've arrived.”

He looked at the vellum, at the single unbroken line crossing it, and then at her, and for once he didn't laugh.
