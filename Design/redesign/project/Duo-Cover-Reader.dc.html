Chapter 7

The Salt Road

The road to the coast had not been mapped in forty years, and Elinor suspected her mother had liked it that way.

She walked it anyway, with the brass compass knocking against her hip and a roll of blank vellum under one arm. Every mile she paused, sighted the dunes, and drew what she saw: the leaning post that had once held a sign, the dry creek bed, the line of gulls that rose whenever the wind changed its mind.

“You’re doing it backwards,” Tomas said, when he caught her up at the third milestone. “You’re supposed to draw the map before you get lost.”

“I’m not lost.” She held up the vellum. “I’m just early.”

He laughed, and the gulls went up again, white against a sky the colour of old pewter.

They walked until the light went copper. Somewhere behind them the dunes had folded themselves away, and ahead there was nothing but the white ground and the long blue shadow each of them threw.

“How will you know where to stop?” Tomas asked.

Elinor checked the compass. The needle was steady, which was its own kind of answer. “When the map runs out,” she said, “I’ll know I’ve arrived.”

He looked at the vellum, at the single unbroken line crossing it, and then at her, and for once he didn’t laugh.
