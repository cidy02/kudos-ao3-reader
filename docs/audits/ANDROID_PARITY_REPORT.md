# Android Parity Review — Report

**Scope:** Independent review of `android/sync-from-hig-review`, the integration branch for Grok's Android work (21 topic branches merged in, spanning three "waves" plus four UI-parity passes). Goal: confirm the Android app is behaviorally sound and matches iOS across every section, catch silent bugs/regressions, and fix what's found.

**Bottom line:** Grok's work is solid overall — the app builds clean, the write-action network layer is a faithful (in one place *more* defensive) port of iOS, and most of what the review surfaced was either already-known/deferred gaps or minor polish differences. But the review did find **10 real bugs — 7 critical, 3 major — all now fixed**, most of them in the "mature content privacy" and "delete/undo" families — the kind of bug that doesn't show up in a demo but matters a lot the first time it bites a real user. It also found 68 additional issues (38 major, 12 minor, 18 note-level) that a single automated pass surfaced but that nobody — human or AI — has independently double-checked yet; see the **Unverified findings** section for the honest accounting of those.

---

## How this review was done (read this before the findings — it explains what "confidence" means below)

1. **Located the work.** 21 `android/*` branches, all merged into `android/sync-from-hig-review` (tip `5ad08eb8` at review start). Reviewed the integration branch rather than each topic branch individually, since that's what any fix lands on anyway.
2. **Read Grok's own audit docs first** (`docs/audits/IOS_ANDROID_DOMAIN_AUDIT.md`, `REMAINING_PARITY_AND_UI_GAPS.md`, `ANDROID_HIG_REVIEW_SYNC.md`) so this review doesn't waste your time re-reporting gaps Grok already knows about and has scheduled. Anything that only restates a documented, deferred gap is filed under **Known, already-tracked gaps**, not presented as a new finding.
3. **Ran a 22-area automated review workflow** (nav, home, library, work detail, download queue, account, settings, browse, search, statistics, collections, reading queues, purge/recently-deleted, author, reader, auth, comments, backup, network-read, network-write, data/persistence, UI components/theme) — each area's findings were meant to go through a second, adversarial "try to prove this wrong" verification pass before being trusted.
4. **The adversarial verification pass failed to complete, twice**, both times from hitting a session/rate limit — 0 of 141 verification checks ever returned a result, across two full attempts. This is the single most important methodology fact in this report: **the 68 lower-severity findings listed later were never independently checked by a second pass, human or AI.** I did not attempt a third expensive re-run of the same workflow; instead I personally, manually verified every `critical`-severity finding against the actual current code before trusting or fixing it (see next point), and ran three additional, narrowly-scoped review passes to cover 5 of the 22 areas whose review agents had silently failed to return anything at all in either run (`backup`, `network-read`, `network-write`, `data`/persistence, `uicomponents`/theme — two of which, backup and network-write, were flagged *before* the review even started as the highest-stakes areas to get right).
5. **Personally re-verified every critical finding against the code**, and found one that looked textbook-solid — specific file, specific line numbers, a "high confidence" tag — and was still wrong on the facts, because the citation pointed at a stale copy of the iOS tree that doesn't have the feature the finding said it was comparing against. The fix was to point at the correct, current iOS tree and recheck; the underlying finding turned out to be *right* after all, just verified against the wrong evidence the first time. This is exactly why unverified findings are labeled unverified below rather than presented as fact — a plausible citation is not the same thing as a checked one.
6. **Fixed every critical finding**, plus 3 more major bugs turned up while fixing them (a data-integrity gap in backup restore for collections, a missing tombstone on reading-queue removal, and a crash-on-malformed-data hardening fix) and one more critical bug the targeted 5-area coverage pass found on its own (a theme picker that visibly does nothing). All fixes are compiled and covered by new unit tests; `android/Scripts/verify.sh` (Grok's own build+test+invariants gate) passes clean.

## Fixed: Critical and major findings

Each finding below has two parts — **Technical** (what's actually wrong in the code, for whoever picks this up next) and **In plain English** (what a user would actually experience).

### 1. Mature content on Home never actually gets hidden

**Technical:** `HomeScreen.kt`'s `HomeWorkCover` composable computed `obscured` correctly but never used it to gate navigation — `onOpen`/`onOpenDetails` always routed to the reader or Work Detail regardless of obscured state (`HomeScreen.kt:350-351` prior to fix), and `WorkDetailScreen.kt` had no privacy handling of its own to catch it on the way in. iOS's equivalent (`MatureContent.swift`) routes a tap on an obscured card to `gate.reveal(work)` first — reveal-in-place, never straight to details.

**In plain English:** If you turned on "hide mature content," Home would still show you a blurred cover with a "tap to reveal" label — but tapping it did nothing to reveal it; it jumped straight into the work's full title, summary, and tags. The blur was purely decorative on Home; it protected nothing.

**Fix:** Added `PrivacyGate` (`app/PrivacyGate.kt`) — a shared, session-only reveal-state object, a direct port of iOS's `PrivacyGate` — and wired it through Home so tapping an obscured card reveals it in place, matching iOS exactly.

### 2. Library's "reveal mature content" toggle permanently changes your privacy setting

**Technical:** `LibraryViewModel.toggleHideMature()` called `settingsRepository.updateHideMatureContent(...)`, which writes the same persisted DataStore key the Settings screen's "Hide mature content" switch controls. iOS's equivalent is a *session-only* reveal-all that resets to hidden on next launch and never touches the underlying setting.

**In plain English:** On iPhone, tapping the eye icon in Library to peek at mature content is temporary — the app goes back to hiding mature content the next time you open it, because your privacy preference itself was never changed. On Android, the same tap silently flipped your actual "hide mature content" setting off — permanently, until you noticed and turned it back on yourself.

**Fix:** Same `PrivacyGate` now backs Library's reveal-all toggle too, so it's session-only and leaves the persisted setting untouched, matching iOS.

### 3. Collections had no mature-content gating at all

**Technical:** `CollectionDetailScreen.kt`'s `CollectionWorkRow` rendered `work.title`/`work.author` and an always-tappable Read button unconditionally — the composable didn't even take a `SettingsRepository`/privacy parameter, unlike every other screen showing local works.

**In plain English:** Every other screen in the app — Home, Library, Reading Insights — respects "hide mature content." Collections was the one place that didn't: opening a collection with a mature work in it showed its full title, author, and a working Read button, completely bypassing the privacy setting you'd turned on.

**Fix:** Wired the same `PrivacyGate`/`LibraryPrivacy` computation into `CollectionDetailScreen.kt` that Library and Home use, so collections now redact and gate exactly like everywhere else.

### 4. Reading Insights ignores "hide mature content" entirely

**Technical:** `ReadingStatisticsViewModel` built its state directly from `libraryRepository.observeSavedWorks()` with zero reference to `LibraryPrivacy`/`PrivacySettings`/`hideMatureContent` anywhere in the file (confirmed via grep — zero hits).

**In plain English:** Turning on "hide mature content" hides adult works everywhere in the app except your reading statistics — your adult-rated works' fandom names, titles, and word counts still showed up in Reading Insights, defeating the point of the privacy setting for anyone who shares a screen or takes a screenshot.

**Fix:** `ReadingStatisticsViewModel` now filters through `LibraryPrivacy.visibility()` before computing statistics, same as Library.

### 5. Deleting a work and getting it back later leaves it stuck in "deleted" limbo

**Technical:** `WorkIdentityIndex.findExisting()` (correctly, by design) matches soft-deleted rows during dedup — but four different call sites that can resurrect a match (`WorkImporter`, `DownloadQueue.isAlreadyDownloaded`, `WorkMetadataMerger`, `WorkDetailScreen.toggleSaved`) didn't clear `isDeleted`/`deletedAt`/`permanentDeletionScheduledAt` when they found one.

**In plain English:** If you deleted a fic (into "Recently Deleted," 90-day undo window) and then later re-downloaded it, re-saved it, or added it to a reading queue — the app *acted* like it worked, but the story silently stayed marked as deleted internally. It could vanish again on its own once the 90-day timer ran out, and it wouldn't reliably show up as a normal saved work in the meantime.

**Fix:** All four revival paths now explicitly clear the soft-delete fields when they match a previously-deleted row, matching iOS's `PreservedWorkService` revival behavior.

### 6. Deleting a collection is instant, permanent, and undoable everywhere except Android

**Technical:** `WorkRepository.deleteCollection()` was a direct, unconditional `DELETE` — no soft-delete step. iOS's equivalent (`PreservedWorkService.softDelete(_ collection:, in:)`) sets `isPendingDeletion`/`deletedAt`/`permanentDeletionScheduledAt` on the collection and keeps it recoverable in Recently Deleted for 90 days, identical to how both platforms already treat deleted *works*. Android's own `RecentlyDeletedScreen.kt` empty-state copy already promised "Deleted works, collections, and reading queues stay here for 90 days" — a promise the app never kept for collections.

*(Note on process: this finding's first citation of the iOS code pointed at a stale, out-of-date copy of the iOS tree bundled inside this Android branch, which made the finding look fabricated on first check. Rechecking against the actual current iOS tree confirmed the finding was correct — see the methodology note above.)*

**In plain English:** On iPhone, deleting a collection (a shelf you made to organize works) is undoable for 90 days, exactly like deleting a work. On Android, one tap and the collection was gone forever with no way to get it back — while the app's own "Recently Deleted" screen was telling you, incorrectly, that it would be recoverable.

**Fix:** Added the same soft-delete/restore/permanent-delete lifecycle Android already has for works — new `lastModifiedAt`/`isDeleted`/`deletedAt`/`permanentDeletionScheduledAt` fields on collections (Room migration v2→v3), a Recently Deleted section that now actually lists deleted collections with Restore/Delete Forever actions, and a 90-day auto-purge sweep on app launch, matching works exactly. Also fixed two related, closely-linked data-integrity bugs this same investigation surfaced (see items #7 and #8 below): collection renames had no conflict resolution during backup restore, and neither collection deletion nor reading-queue-item removal recorded the "tombstone" marker the sync system needs to avoid silently un-deleting things on restore.

### 7. Backup restore could silently revert a collection rename, or resurrect a deleted collection

**Technical:** `BackupMergeService.mergeCollections()` unconditionally overwrote a local collection's name/fields with whatever the archive said, with no last-write-wins timestamp check — unlike every other mergeable type in the same file (works, reading queues, annotations all gate updates by `SyncMerge.shouldApplyIncoming`). And because collections had no soft-delete state (see #6) and no tombstone recorded on deletion, restoring an older backup that still listed a deleted collection would silently bring it back.

**In plain English:** If you renamed a collection on your phone and then restored an older backup (say, after getting a new device), your rename could get silently overwritten by the older name from the backup — with no indication anything happened. And if you'd deleted a collection, restoring almost any backup would bring it right back, because the app had no memory that you'd deleted it.

**Fix:** Collection merges are now gated by the same last-write-wins comparison every other synced type uses, and collection deletion now records the same "tombstone" marker works already use, so a stale backup can't resurrect something you deliberately deleted.

### 8. Removing a work from a reading queue could be silently undone by a backup restore

**Technical:** `ReadingQueueRepository.removeWork()` deleted the membership row directly with no tombstone recorded, even though the merge logic (`TombstoneIndex.membershipResolution`) already expects and checks for one — reading queues already had the tombstone *consuming* side wired up, just not the *producing* side for this one action.

**In plain English:** If you removed a story from a reading queue (like "Saved for Later") and later restored a backup that still listed it, the story could quietly reappear in that queue — because the app never recorded that you'd taken it out.

**Fix:** `removeWork` now records a tombstone the same way work and collection deletion do.

### 9. Settings' theme picker doesn't control the app's actual theme

**Technical:** The Settings screen's theme picker (Light/Sepia/Dark/OLED/System) correctly persisted its choice to DataStore — but `KudosApp.kt`'s `themeMode`, the value that actually feeds `MaterialTheme`'s color scheme, was local Compose state initialized to `System` on every process start and never read the persisted value at all. The app's real, working theme control turned out to be a separate, undocumented palette icon in the top bar, cycling through a different 4-value enum with no persistence of its own.

**In plain English:** Picking "Dark" in Settings visibly did nothing — the screen just sat there unchanged. Meanwhile a small, unlabeled palette icon elsewhere on the same screen actually did change the app's colors, but that choice was forgotten every time you closed and reopened the app. Two controls, only one of which worked, and the one that worked didn't remember your choice.

**Fix:** `KudosApp.kt` now reads the persisted theme from Settings and both controls write to and read from the same source of truth — picking a theme in Settings now works and survives a restart, and the palette icon stays in sync with it.

### 10. A single corrupted tag can crash the whole Library screen

**Technical:** `KudosTypeConverters.jsonToStringList` (used for 7 different `List<String>` columns on every saved work — warnings, categories, tags, fandoms, etc.) had no error handling around JSON decoding. One row with malformed JSON — from an interrupted write, a future format change, anything — would throw uncaught and crash any query that touched it, including a plain Library list load.

**In plain English:** This is a "just in case" fix rather than something known to have happened — but as written, one damaged row in the database could take down your entire Library screen instead of just that one story failing to display correctly.

**Fix:** Malformed JSON now degrades to an empty list instead of throwing.

### App shell, navigation, tab structure

**[Major — already fixed, see #9 above]** `app/KudosApp.kt`
- **Technical:** iOS persists the whole-app theme through `ThemeManager.appTheme` (App/ThemeManager.swift:13-14, backed by UserDefaults, ContentView.swift:74 `.preferredColorScheme(theme.appTheme.colorScheme)` drives the actual rendered appearance). Android has an equivalent persisted setting, `AppThemeSetting`, written via `SettingsRepository.updateAppTheme` (data/preferences/SettingsRepository.kt:34-36) and edited from a Settings screen picker with Light/Sepia/Dark/OLED/System chips (settings/SettingsScreen.kt:280-312, whose footer literally says 'Light, Sepia, Dark, or System across the app'). However, `KudosTheme(...)` — the single `MaterialTheme` wrapper that actually determines every color in the app (ui/theme/Theme.kt:66-84) — is called exactly once in the whole codebase, from `KudosApp.kt:15`, and is driven by a completely separate, in-memory-only `KudosThemeMode` enum (`var themeMode by remember { mutableStateOf(KudosThemeMode.System) }`, KudosApp.kt:13) that is only ever mutated by the small palette icon shown on Account/Settings (MainScaffold.kt:104-112, `onCycleTheme`). `SettingsRepository`'s persisted `appTheme` is read only by `ReaderSettingsMapper` (for the reader) and `BackupSettings`; it never reaches `KudosTheme`. Net effect: picking Light/Sepia/Dark/OLED in Settings persists the choice and visibly updates the chip selection, but does nothing to the app's actual rendered colors; the app's real (unlabeled, unpersisted) theme control is the palette icon, which resets to System on every process restart.
- **In plain English:** The Settings screen has a normal-looking theme picker (Light / Sepia / Dark / OLED / System) that saves your choice but never actually changes how the app looks — the app is colored by a different, hidden control (a small palette icon that only appears on the Account/Settings screen) that isn't saved at all, so it resets to System every time you reopen the app. A user who picks Sepia in Settings will see nothing change, and even the palette-icon workaround won't stick between launches.

**[Major]** `app/AppNavHost.kt`
- **Technical:** iOS gives each tab its own typed `NavigationStack(path: $path)` with `.navigationDestination(for: Type.self)` (e.g. HomeView.swift:106,123-131; LibraryView.swift:128,143-150) — every pushed screen carries its own associated data (the specific `SavedWork`/`AO3WorkSummary`/etc.) as part of the path element itself, so there is never ambiguity about which work a given stack entry shows. Android's `AppNavHost` instead uses a single flat `NavHost` where every destination route (`work-detail`, `reader`, `author-works`, `comments`, ...) takes no arguments, and 'which item is currently selected' is tracked by app-scoped `remember { mutableStateOf(...) }` variables declared once at AppNavHost.kt:55-64 (`selectedWorkSource`, `readerWorkId`, `selectedAuthorName`, etc.) that are shared by every call site and never reset on back-navigation. Concretely: Home's `onOpenWork` sets `selectedWorkSource = LocalWork(A)` and pushes `Routes.WorkDetail` (AppNavHost.kt:78-81); from that Work Detail screen tapping the author pushes Author Works (`onOpenAuthor`, AppNavHost.kt:394-397); from Author Works tapping a different work by that author sets `selectedWorkSource = RemoteSummary(B)` and pushes `Routes.WorkDetail` again (AppNavHost.kt:408-411) — none of these `navigate()` calls use `launchSingleTop`, so the back stack now legitimately contains two separate `work-detail` entries. Pressing back twice returns the user to the *first* `work-detail` entry, but `composable(Routes.WorkDetail)` re-executes and reads the *current* value of `selectedWorkSource`, which is still `RemoteSummary(B)` — the screen the user originally left (Work A) is gone; Work B is shown instead. The same shared-variable pattern applies to `readerWorkId`, `selectedCommentTarget`, `selectedAccountListType`, `selectedBrowseCategory/Fandom`, `webFallbackUrl`, `selectedQueueId`, `selectedCollectionId`, and is further compounded by `MainScaffold.kt`'s bottom-nav `navigateTopLevel` (lines 217-225), which uses the standard Compose 'multiple back stacks' `popUpTo(...){saveState=true}/restoreState=true` recipe — restoring a saved per-tab back stack re-renders whichever of these singleton-route destinations it contains using whatever the shared variable happens to hold at that moment, not what it held when that tab's entry was originally pushed.
- **In plain English:** Ordinary browsing can leave you looking at the wrong work. Example: open a work from Home, tap its author to see their other works, then open a different work from that list. Now press back twice — instead of landing back on the work you started from, you land back on the second work you just visited, because the app only remembers 'the most recently opened work' globally rather than remembering which work belonged to which screen in your back history. The same issue affects going back to a previously-opened Reader, Comments thread, Author page, Reading Queue, Collection, or Browse fandom/work list whenever more than one of that same kind of screen has been opened along the way.

### Authentication / login

**[Major]** `auth/AO3WebLoginScreen.kt`
- **Technical:** AO3WebLoginScreen's WebViewClient.shouldOverrideUrlLoading guards navigation with `!request.url.host.orEmpty().endsWith(AO3Constants.WORKS_HOST)`, i.e. a bare `endsWith("archiveofourown.org")` with no domain-boundary check. That allows any host that merely ends with the literal string, e.g. `evilarchiveofourown.org` or `xarchiveofourown.org`, to load inside the login WebView (shouldOverrideUrlLoading returns false = let it load) — the malicious host is a strict superstring, not a real subdomain. Every other AO3-host check in this codebase gets this right by requiring a dot before the suffix: AO3CookieJar.kt:24 (`normalized.endsWith(".${'$'}{AO3Constants.WORKS_HOST}")`), AO3Session.kt/AO3StoredCookie.isAO3Domain:49 (same pattern), and iOS's AO3RequestDefaults.isTrustedURL in AO3AuthService.swift:213 (`host.hasSuffix(".archiveofourown.org")`). Only this one call site is missing the leading dot, so it's an inconsistency within the same codebase, not a deliberate design choice.
- **In plain English:** The login screen's check for 'is this still an AO3 page' can be tricked by a domain that simply ends with the letters 'archiveofourown.org' with nothing separating them, like 'evilarchiveofourown.org' — it isn't required to be a real archiveofourown.org subdomain. If an attacker ever got the login page to redirect there, the app would treat it as trusted and let it render full-screen inside the login flow, which is exactly the kind of check that's supposed to stop that.

**[Major]** `auth/AO3AuthRepository.kt`
- **Technical:** AO3AuthRepository.restoreSession() calls `sessionStore.load()` (line 33) with no try/catch around it. FileAO3SessionStore.load() (AO3SessionStore.kt:24-35) only catches IllegalArgumentException and SerializationException around `sessionFile.readText()` — a plain IOException (e.g. MalformedInputException from a partially-written/corrupted file, a permission error, or a disk I/O failure) is not caught and propagates out of restoreSession(). restoreSession() is invoked from AccountViewModel.kt:28 as `viewModelScope.launch { authRepository.restoreSession() }`, which also has no try/catch, so an uncaught IOException here crashes the app on launch. iOS's equivalent restoreSession() (AO3AuthService.swift:401-428) wraps the whole `vault.load()` call in a do/catch that maps every error (not just deserialization errors) to a safe `.signedOut` state with a message, and even has a dedicated recovery path for a missing-Keychain-entitlement error.
- **In plain English:** If the saved-login file on disk is ever unreadable for a reason other than being garbled JSON — a permission glitch, a disk error, a half-written file from a previous crash — the Android app will crash every time it starts, instead of just showing the user as logged out like the iPhone app does in the same situation.

**[Major]** `auth/AO3WebLoginScreen.kt`
- **Technical:** In AO3WebLoginScreen's onPageFinished (lines 88-103), the JS inspection result is only acted on when `inspection.loggedIn && inspection.username != null`; if loggedIn is true but the `#greeting a[href^='/users/']` scrape fails to find a username (markup change, A/B variant, edge case), the branch is simply skipped — no call to acceptWebLogin, no error message set, no state change. The screen just sits there, fully loaded, silently never completing. - **Technical:** WorkRepository.observeSavedWorks() (WorkRepository.kt:38-41) filters the entire work list down to isSaved == true, and LibraryRepository.observeSnapshot() (LibraryRepository.kt:14-32) builds the whole Library dashboard exclusively from that filtered stream — so a work with isSaved == false is invisible to every Library shelf (Reading Now, Saved for Later, Finished, Downloaded, History), not just 'Saved for Later'. ### Recently Deleted / purge of expired soft-deletes

**[Major]** `works/WorkMetadataMerger.kt`
- **Technical:** Where Android's revival logic does fire — `WorkMetadataMerger.merge()` clearing `isDeleted`/`deletedAt`/`permanentDeletionScheduledAt` when `existing.isDeleted == true` (WorkMetadataMerger.kt:39, 82-84), used by `WorkImporter.saveMetadataOnly`/`download` and reached from the everyday 'Save' button (WorkDetailScreen.kt:245-253) — it never retracts the `SyncTombstone` recorded at soft-delete time, and never bumps `lastModifiedAt` either (the `base.copy(...)` in WorkMetadataMerger.kt omits `lastModifiedAt`, so it stays at the original deletion timestamp). `WorkRepository.upsert()` (WorkRepository.kt:87-90), which this path calls, does not retract tombstones — only `restoreFromRecentlyDeleted()` (WorkRepository.kt:175-187, via `retractWorkTombstone` at 252-254) does that. `DownloadQueue.isAlreadyDownloaded()` (DownloadQueue.kt:240-253) works around this for its own call site only, and its comment explicitly says so: '...restoreFromRecentlyDeleted also retracts the soft-delete's sync tombstone, which the WorkImporter-side revival fixes for the redownload paths don't (they have no repository access to do so).' Consequence: `BackupMergeService.suppressesWorkResurrection` (BackupMergeService.kt:742-758) suppresses an incoming work when `tombstone.lastModifiedAt >= archivedWork.lastModifiedAt`. Since a merger-revived work keeps `lastModifiedAt` pinned at the original soft-delete instant (same instant the tombstone recorded), a subsequent backup export/import onto a new device (or merge with a second device) will treat the tombstone as authoritative and silently drop the now-active, revived work forever — even though it's alive and visible on the originating device. iOS's `PreservedWorkService.restore` (PreservedWorkService.swift:70-77) always retracts the tombstone and is called unconditionally by the revive-on-any-interaction path in `importEPUB` (WorkImporter.swift:39-40) and `ReadingQueueService.addToSavedForLater`; this is explicitly asserted by `PreservedWorkTests.swift` (`addToSavedForLaterRevivesRecentlyDeletedWork`, line 262; `importEPUBRevivesRecentlyDeletedRecordInsteadOfDuplicating`, lines 266-291), which Android's test suite has no equivalent of.
- **In plain English:** When you delete a work and then re-save it later (not through the explicit Restore button), the app correctly brings it back into your Library on this device — but it leaves behind an internal 'this was deleted' marker that never gets cleaned up. If you later back up your library and restore it on a new phone (or sync with another device), that leftover marker can cause the work to silently vanish again on the new device, permanently, with no indication anything went wrong.

**[Major]** `works/WorkRepository.kt`

### Search screen

**[Major]** `search/SearchScreen.kt`
- **Technical:** iOS's SearchView.swift (lines 206-247, 311-451) implements a debounced, on-device 'local-first' search: as the user types into `filters.query` with no explicit submit, `refreshLocalMatches()`/`computeLocalMatches()` scan `WorkSearchIndex` over saved works, library fandom names, the cached AO3 fandom catalog, user tags, and collections, and render them live in `localResultsList` (sections 'In Your Library', 'Fandoms in Your Library', 'Fandoms on AO3', 'Your Tags', 'Collections') alongside an explicit 'Search AO3 for …' action — before any network request fires. Android's SearchScreen.kt has no equivalent: `state` only transitions among `Idle` (saved-searches list or a static prompt, lines 219-240), `Loading`, `Error`, and `Results`, all driven exclusively by `AO3SearchRepository.search()` (network). Typing into the query field (lines 178-184) does nothing locally; `works`/`userTagNames`/`localTagSuggestions` (lines 69-77) exist only to feed autocomplete candidates inside the filter sheet's `TagSuggestField`s (SearchFilterSheet.kt), never to surface an on-device results list. - **In plain English:** On iPhone, typing a few letters into Search instantly shows matches from your own library (saved works, fandoms, tags, collections) before it ever touches the network — and tapping a tag anywhere in the app jumps straight into a live Search for it. On Android, the Search screen only ever talks to AO3: typing does nothing until you press "Search," there's no way to see your own saved works/tags/collections from this screen, and tags elsewhere in the app aren't tappable to search at all.

**[Major]** `search/SearchScreen.kt`
- **Technical:** `clearFilters()` (SearchScreen.kt:130-132) only replaces `filters` with `clearedFiltersPreservingQuery(filters)`; it never touches `state`. If `state` is currently `SearchUiState.Results` (or `Error`) from a filtered search, tapping either Clear button removes the active-filter chip row and drops the Filters badge to 0, but the results list on screen keeps showing the stale, still-filtered page — nothing re-runs the search or resets to idle. - **In plain English:** Tapping "Clear" on the Search screen removes the filter chips from view, but the work list underneath still shows the old, filtered results — it doesn't actually refresh. A user would reasonably think clearing filters also cleared the results, but they have to press Search again to get results that match what's now on screen. In the worst case (clearing leaves nothing left to search), the screen gets stuck showing stale results with the Search button greyed out.

**[Major]** `android/app/src/main/java/io/github/cidy02/kudos/search/SearchScreen.kt`

- **In plain English:** On iPhone, as soon as you start typing in Search you immediately see matches from your own downloaded library (works, fandoms, tags, collections) before any request even goes to AO3. On Android, typing does nothing until you explicitly tap Search, and that search only ever looks at AO3, never your own library. Users can no longer find something they already have downloaded by typing its title, fandom, or tag into Search.

**[Major]** `android/app/src/main/java/io/github/cidy02/kudos/search/TagSuggestField.kt`

If the user's downloaded library is empty or lacks the desired tag, the fandom/character/relationship/additional-tags filter fields on Android offer zero suggestions and there is no way to browse valid AO3 tag spellings at all.
- **In plain English:** On iPhone, when you fill in a Fandom/Character/Relationship/Tag filter, you get a live search of all of AO3's tags plus a list of that fandom's most popular tags, so you can find the exact tag even if you've never downloaded a matching work. On Android, tag suggestions only ever come from works you've already saved locally - if your library is empty or doesn't have that tag, you get no suggestions at all and have to know the exact AO3 tag spelling yourself.

### Work Detail screen

**[Major]** `works/WorkUpdateChecker.kt`
- **Technical:** In WorkUpdateChecker.checkForUpdates (works/WorkUpdateChecker.kt:35-39), the AO3Result.Failure branch does `continue` without updating `work.lastUpdateCheck`, so a work whose AO3 fetch fails (network error, locked/orphaned/deleted work, rate limit) never gets `lastUpdateCheck` stamped. Since `shouldCheck()` (line 53-58) gates re-polling purely on `Duration.between(lastUpdateCheck, now) >= MinInterval`, a permanently-failing work is 'due' again on every single call to `checkForUpdates` (e.g. every Home load), forever. iOS's equivalent (Services/WorkUpdateChecker.swift:34-40) explicitly stamps `work.lastUpdateCheck = Date()` in the catch block specifically 'so a failing work waits out `minInterval` instead of being retried on every Home visit' — the exact scenario Android fails to guard against. Both files' docstrings claim the same 'polite by design... throttled' behavior, but only iOS's implementation actually delivers it for the failure path.
- **In plain English:** When Kudos checks a saved in-progress story for new chapters and that check fails (locked story, deleted story, or just a network hiccup), the app is supposed to wait 6 hours before trying that story again. On Android it doesn't — a story that keeps failing gets re-checked against AO3 every single time you open Home, instead of at most every few hours like the iOS app does.

**[Major]** `works/WorkDetailScreen.kt`
- **Technical:** Android's Work Detail screen never re-fetches AO3 metadata for an already-saved work. `LaunchedEffect(source)` (WorkDetailScreen.kt:198-235) for `WorkDetailSource.LocalWork` only calls `refreshLocal()` (143-154), which reads the local DB — no network call. There is no pull-to-refresh and no equivalent of iOS's `.task { … WorkTags.refreshFromAO3(for: work, in: context) }` (WorkDetailView.swift:160-172) or `.refreshable { await refreshDetails() }` (144-145, 576-595). On Android, none of that happens — once you've saved a story, its kudos/comments/hits/tag numbers are stuck at whatever they were when you first downloaded it, and there's no button or gesture anywhere on the screen (not even 'Redownload EPUB') that updates them.

## Reviewed and confirmed clean

These areas were part of the 5 that never returned results from the original 22-area workflow (session-limit exhaustion), so they got dedicated, targeted follow-up review rather than being left as an unexamined gap. Unlike everything above, these results **were** produced by focused single-area review agents with narrower scope and are reported here at the same confidence as the rest of this document — i.e. not adversarially re-verified, but not blind either.

**Network-write (kudos, comments, subscribe, bookmark POST paths):** No critical or major issues. Every write action correctly inspects the actual HTTP response — status code, login-redirect, AO3's overload/maintenance page, and body-level error text — before declaring success; nothing here treats "the request didn't throw" as "it worked." One genuinely interesting result: Android's redirect/cookie-relay handling (`AO3RedirectCookieRelay.kt`) is **more correct than iOS's**, which has no equivalent logic at all and could in principle carry a stale session cookie across a redirect. This isn't currently a live bug on either platform (neither platform's success detection depends on it), but it's worth knowing Android is ahead here, not behind. One minor robustness note: `WorkDetailScreen.kt`'s `runWorkAction` wrapper doesn't catch exceptions thrown by the action itself, which could leave the UI stuck in a "working" state rather than showing an error — low risk today since every actual failure path already returns a typed result rather than throwing, but worth hardening.

**Network-read (shared HTTP client — coalescing, retry, overload handling):** No critical bugs. Request coalescing (avoiding duplicate concurrent fetches of the same URL) and retry/backoff match iOS closely. Two minor, harmless differences: Android retries on any `IOException` where iOS only retries a curated list of transient error codes (a latency nuisance on a permanently-broken connection, not a correctness issue), and Android has an overload-page detector iOS lacks — which, incidentally, means Android is *more* defensive against a failure mode (a 200-status "server's busy" page silently parsing as "zero results") that iOS is actually more exposed to.

**Data/persistence (Room database core, DataStore settings):** One major finding, already listed under **Fixed** above (#10, the malformed-JSON crash). Room migrations are correct and non-destructive — no `fallbackToDestructiveMigration` anywhere, and the existing v1→v2 migration properly backfills rather than dropping data (the new v2→v3 migration added by this review's collections fix follows the same pattern). One minor note: `readerLetterSpacing`/`readerWordSpacing` settings exist in the model and round-trip through backup, but have no live UI control anywhere in the app yet to actually change them day-to-day — not a bug, just a reminder for whoever wires up that UI.

## Known, already-tracked gaps (not new findings)

These came up during review — either surfaced by an agent or noticed in passing — but they're already documented and scheduled in Grok's own audit trail (`docs/audits/REMAINING_PARITY_AND_UI_GAPS.md`, `IOS_ANDROID_DOMAIN_AUDIT.md`), so they're listed here for completeness rather than presented as something newly discovered:

- Non-EPUB (PDF/HTML/txt) import — Android is EPUB-only by design for now.
- Folder / iCloud-style sync — Android path explicitly "TBD" in Grok's own doc.
- Full author profile pages (bio, Subscribe/Mute/Block, tabbed Works/Series/Bookmarks) and a real Activity/Inbox — both confirmed still genuinely missing on Android (not just under a different name) by this review; tracked as known gaps, not new findings.
- Reader TOC/chrome polish, font customization depth (letter/word spacing, live preview), and text-to-speech — known, deferred.
- Biometric reveal for mature works — missing on Android, known gap. Note: the underlying gating (blur/hide, independent of the biometric step) does now work correctly on every screen after this review's fixes — see items #1–#4 above.
- Loading skeletons for Search/Browse/Account lists — only the reader got one so far; known, deferred.
- Liquid Glass / peel-dismiss / zoom-transition chrome — iOS-only by design, never a parity target.
- The "remote card downloads-then-opens-in-reader" tap rule (`f1f2844` on iOS) — ported for already-local cards; the remote-card case is Grok's own documented next task.

## Recommendations

1. **Don't re-run the same 22-way adversarial verification workflow a third time as-is.** It hit the same session-limit ceiling twice with the same shape. If the ~90 findings above need independent verification, it's worth doing in smaller batches (5-8 areas at a time) rather than one large fan-out.
2. **The 5 areas covered late (backup, network-write, network-read, persistence, theme) deserve the same adversarial scrutiny as the rest**, precisely because they were rushed in as a follow-up rather than reviewed on the original schedule. The findings in those areas are reported at the same "single unverified pass" confidence as everything else, not higher.
3. **Prioritize the "App shell, navigation, tab structure" major finding** (back-stack navigation can land you on the wrong work after visiting several detail-type screens in a row) if you tackle one more thing from the unverified list — it's the kind of bug that's easy to hit by accident and confusing when it happens, and unlike most findings here it has no existing test coverage protecting the exact code paths involved.
4. One repo-hygiene item noticed in passing, not touched: `git fetch --all` reported a corrupted ref name (`android/sync-from-hig-review 2`, with a literal trailing space, both locally and on `origin`). Worth a `git update-ref -d` / a look at whatever created it, at your convenience — not urgent, not touched here since ref deletion wasn't something to do unprompted.
